# Earth Radiation Geometry (Dice Satellite Model)

## Overview

This example examines the directions from which Earth infrared radiation can reach a spacecraft radiator during orbital operation. A simplified *dice satellite* model is used before introducing the detailed geometry of the radiator and its thermal shields.

The purpose is to identify incident directions relevant to radiator and shield design, and to examine their dependence on orbital phase, spacecraft attitude, and season (represented by solar ecliptic longitude).

**This is a directional geometrical coupling study, not a calculation of absolute thermal load in watts.**

## Method

The calculation uses 73 spacecraft positions spanning one orbital revolution (72 intervals) and 200,000 approximately uniformly distributed directions generated by a Fibonacci distribution. At each orbital position, directions in the spacecraft +Z hemisphere are tested against the apparent Earth disk. The positive Z components of directions intersecting the Earth are accumulated to form a directional exposure map.

The accumulated quantity is a cosine-weighted directional exposure count. It is **not normalized to a physical view factor** and is not converted into radiative power.

The maps use spacecraft coordinates: +Z is the north pole, longitude 0° corresponds to −X, and elevations range from 0° to 90° above the XY plane.

| Parameter | Value |

## Results and Design Implications

- Earth coupling is particularly significant from directions near the spacecraft **±Y axes**.
- For the examined conditions, Earth radiation can reach elevations approaching **70° above the radiator plane**.
- Under some seasonal conditions, coupling also occurs near the **−X direction** at elevations exceeding approximately **20°**.
- Shortening the observation interval near its boundaries can reduce the highest Earth-incidence elevations, at the cost of observing time.

These results motivate the study of reflective shield geometries, particularly for radiation arriving at high elevation angles from the ±Y directions.

## Scope and Limitations

The model does not include detailed spacecraft surfaces, spectral radiance, surface absorptivity or emissivity, reflection, or conductive heat transfer. The Earth is treated as a spherical disk of uniform angular extent.

Therefore, these maps should be interpreted as **directional screening information for radiator and shield design**, not as final thermal-load predictions.
